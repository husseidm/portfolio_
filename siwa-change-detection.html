Environmental Change Detection Using GIS and Remote Sensing

A Multi-Indicator Approach to Environmental Change Analysis Using Satellite-Derived Spectral Indices and GIS.

This project maps four environmental indicators for Siwa Oasis in 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024: vegetation (NDVI), surface water (MNDWI), water turbidity (NDTI) and surface water temperature. Each indicator is presented as a complete ArcGIS Pro layout, with yearly maps, change maps where available, and a study area inset.

Project Overview

Project overview

Satellite imagery records how a landscape reflects and emits energy. A GIS turns those measurements into maps that can be compared from one year to the next. This project combines both to examine environmental conditions in Siwa Oasis across four observation years between 2014 and 2024. According to the layouts, the aim is to assess environmental and agricultural change over the ten-year period and to identify areas where vegetation, water resources, turbidity and surface temperature changed noticeably.

Four indicators are used. Each describes a different environmental property, so they are read side by side and are not interchangeable.

NDVIDensity and vigour of green vegetation(NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red)
MNDWIOpen surface water, separated from other surfaces(Green − SWIR) / (Green + SWIR)

NDVI

Vegetation Distribution and Change Analysis

What NDVI measures. The Normalized Difference Vegetation Index compares near-infrared and red reflectance. Healthy vegetation reflects strongly in the near-infrared and absorbs red light, so higher NDVI values generally indicate denser, greener vegetation. Water and bare surfaces give low or negative values.

NDVI = (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red)Standard definition. Band numbers: [to be added once the sensor is confirmed]

What the layout shows

  • Main maps: NDVI for 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024, drawn on a continuous ramp from red-orange (low) through yellow to dark green (high). The legend runs from −0.423 to 0.225.
  • Difference maps: NDVI change for 2014 & 2018 and for 2024 & 2021, classified as decrease (yellow), stable (orange) and increase (red).
  • Charts: vegetation area in km² for each year (2.43, 1.45, 1.14 and 0.65 for 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024) and the highest and lowest NDVI value for each year, as plotted.
  • Study area: the Map Location inset in the lower left.

Observed patterns

The 2014 and 2018 maps are dominated by orange tones, with greener values along parts of the western margin. In 2021 the extent shifts toward yellow, and in 2024 most of it is yellow to yellow-green. The 2014 & 2018 change map has a large red area across the north-eastern and central parts and some yellow patches near the margins. In the 2024 & 2021 change map almost the whole extent falls in the stable class, with small red and yellow areas at the northern tip.

Because the chart plots vegetation area while the maps show index values, the two should be read as different measures. How the area values were derived [to be confirmed].

Reading the legend

The NDVI values in this layout stay low across the whole extent (maximum 0.225). Interpretation should therefore focus on relative differences between years and areas rather than on absolute vegetation density.

MNDWI

Water Distribution and Change Analysis

Purpose. The second layout uses the Modified Normalized Difference Water Index (MNDWI), not the original NDWI. MNDWI replaces the near-infrared band with a shortwave-infrared band, which separates open water from built-up and bare surfaces more reliably. Higher values indicate open water.

MNDWI = (Green − SWIR) / (Green + SWIR)Standard definition. Band numbers: [to be added once the sensor is confirmed]

What the layout shows

  • Main maps: MNDWI for 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024 on a blue ramp. The legend runs from −0.221 to 0.463.
  • Difference maps: MNDWI change for 2014 & 2018 and for 2024 & 2021, classified as decrease (yellow), stable (orange) and increase (red) in the water surface.
  • Charts: a water area chart in km² (25.8, 26.5, 26.7 and 26.6 for 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024) and the highest and lowest MNDWI value for each year, as plotted.

Observed patterns

In all four years most of the extent is light to mid blue, with darker blue along parts of the margin and in a few small interior spots. The plotted water area varies by less than 1 km² across the four years. The 2014 & 2018 change map shows large yellow (decrease) patches in the north-east and centre, with a small red (increase) area at the western tip. The 2024 & 2021 change map is mostly stable, with small red and yellow areas at the north and west.

NDTI

Water Turbidity Patterns and Change Analysis

Purpose. The Normalized Difference Turbidity Index (NDTI) uses the contrast between red and green reflectance. Turbid water, which carries suspended sediment, reflects relatively more red light, so higher NDTI values are associated with higher turbidity. The layout text describes the index as being based on the red and green bands.

NDTI = (Red − Green) / (Red + Green)Standard definition, consistent with the red and green bands stated in the layout. Band numbers: [to be added once the sensor is confirmed]

What the layout shows

  • Main maps: NDTI for 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024, classified into three turbidity classes: clear water (yellow), medium turbidity (orange) and high turbidity (red).
  • Difference maps: NDTI change for 2014 & 2018 and for 2024 & 2021. The legend is titled “Map Change 2018 & 2014” and uses decrease in turbidity (yellow), stable (orange) and a red class labelled high turbidity.
  • Chart: mean NDTI for each year, plotted as −49, −63, −72 and −106 for 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024. The scaling of these values [to be confirmed].

Observed patterns

The 2014 map is mostly in the clear class, with medium turbidity along parts of the margin and red along some edges. In 2018 and 2021 the medium class covers a larger part of the western and central area. By 2024 almost the whole extent falls in the clear class, with narrow medium and high classes at some edges. The two change maps both show extensive non-stable classes, with the larger red area in the centre and north-east on the 2014 & 2018 map.

Limits of NDTI

NDTI is a relative proxy derived from reflectance. It is not a laboratory measurement of turbidity. Its values are affected by water depth, bottom reflectance, shoreline mixing, atmospheric conditions and the dissolved or suspended material present, and they are not calibrated against in-situ turbidity samples unless that was done [state whether any field data was used]. The classes should be read as relative patterns between years and locations.

Change Detection

Reading the change maps

Change detection compares the same indicator at two dates. Three different things are easy to confuse.

  • Index values describe the condition in one year, for example how green or how wet a pixel is in 2014.
  • The difference is the result of subtracting one year's values from another's, pixel by pixel. It states how much the value moved and in which direction, and nothing more.
  • The environmental interpretation is a separate step. It depends on what the indicator represents and on what is known about the area and the dates.

A positive difference is not automatically an improvement, and a negative difference is not automatically degradation. The meaning depends on the indicator, the order of subtraction, the chosen years and the environmental context. For turbidity and temperature, a rise is not good or bad in itself. For water, an increase in the index may reflect a real change in water extent or a change in conditions on the acquisition dates.

What the supplied layouts provide

  • Change maps for NDVI, MNDWI and NDTI, for the pairs labelled 2014 & 2018 and 2024 & 2021. There is no 2018–2021 pair and no 2014–2024 map.
  • Changes are shown in three classes: decrease (yellow), stable (orange) and increase (red). The class thresholds are not shown.
  • The temperature layout has yearly maps and a chart of yearly means, and no difference map.

Results

Key findings

These points describe what is visible in the layouts. They are observations of patterns, and they do not establish causes.

Vegetation

  • NDVI values stay low across the extent (legend maximum 0.225).
  • The 2014 and 2018 maps are mostly orange, 2021 shifts toward yellow, and 2024 is mostly yellow to yellow-green.
  • The chart plots vegetation area of 2.43, 1.45, 1.14 and 0.65 km² for 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024.

Water

  • Most of the extent is light to mid blue in every year, with darker values near some margins.
  • The plotted water area changes by less than 1 km² across the four years (25.8 to 26.7 km²).
  • The plotted maximum MNDWI declines from 0.46 in 2014 to 0.31 in 2024.

Turbidity

  • The medium turbidity class covers more of the western and central area in 2018 and 2021 than in 2014.
  • The 2024 map is almost entirely in the clear class.
  • The plotted mean NDTI values decline from −49 in 2014 to −106 in 2024, in the layout's own scaling.

Temperature

  • The plotted yearly means are 17.09, 14.69, 18.27 and 13.38, falling, rising and falling again.
  • The spatial pattern differs from year to year, with a distinct dark band across the north-east and centre in 2024.
  • Blue (highest legend values) appears along parts of the margin in every year.

Changes between observation periods

  • For NDVI and MNDWI, the 2014 & 2018 change maps show large non-stable areas in the north-east and centre, while the 2024 & 2021 maps are mostly stable.
  • The NDTI change maps show extensive non-stable classes in both periods.
  • The temperature layout contains no difference map.
  • What the increase and decrease classes mean in each case depends on the subtraction order, which is still to be confirmed.

Conclusion

Conclusion

Each indicator in this study describes a different property of Siwa Oasis: vegetation through NDVI, open water through MNDWI, relative turbidity through NDTI and the thermal state of the water surface through temperature. Viewed together across 2014, 2018, 2021 and 2024, they give a multi-dimensional picture that no single index provides.

The maps show where and how values differ between years. They do not by themselves establish why. Explaining the causes would need additional data, such as climate records, land-use information or field measurements, and a confirmed account of the acquisition dates and processing.
